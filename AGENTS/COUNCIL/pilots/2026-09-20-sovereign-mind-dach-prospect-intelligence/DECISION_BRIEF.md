## Recommended Approach

Run the first Sovereign Mind prospect-intelligence campaign as a diagnostic customer-conversation campaign, not as an "AI for manufacturing" lead list.

Start with DACH packaging machinery, food/pharma process-line, filling, inspection, labeling, end-of-line automation, and adjacent service-heavy production-equipment builders. Use large recognizable companies as benchmark accounts to learn the language of the market, then prioritize reachable mid-market and hidden-champion firms for actual outreach when their public evidence is stronger.

The output should be:

- A ranked list of 25-40 DACH targets.
- 10-15 priority dossiers ready for Miguel/Reto review.
- A reusable dossier schema and public-source ledger.
- A conversation guide and 3-5 claim-safe outreach angles.

Success should be measured by 8-12 exploratory conversations with service, aftermarket, digital service, sales engineering, product, customer success, or operations leaders.

## Why This Approach

The council converged on one central point: Sovereign Mind will get better conversations by leading with customer pain, not with technology.

Relevant market context supports a pain-led investigation:

- VDMA says German machinery production is expected to contract by 2 percent in real terms in 2026, the fourth consecutive year of decline, with 2027 growth expected. Source: https://www.vdma.eu/en/economic-situation
- Swissmem says the Swiss tech industry recovery remains fragile, with pressure on SMEs, margins, exports, and capacity utilisation. Source: https://www.swissmem.ch/en/media-corner/media-releases/recovery-in-the-tech-industry-remains-fragile.html
- CECIMO describes the European machine-tool sector as operating in a difficult context with weak demand, global competition, trade risks, and a fragile recovery. Source: https://www.cecimo.eu/news/cecimo-press-release-economic-recovery-remains-fragile-amid-persistent-structural-pressures/
- CECIMO also highlights cyber-regulatory complexity for machine tools under the Cyber Resilience Act. Source: https://www.cecimo.eu/publications/the-cra-complexities-for-the-machine-tool-industry/
- Sovereign Mind's product reality is strongest where field or industrial teams need source-grounded, offline knowledge access and refusal instead of confabulation. Source: `../sovereign-mind/README.md`

The outreach hypothesis should therefore be:

Machine builders with complex installed bases may have customer-facing friction around service knowledge, documentation, training, spare parts, commissioning, maintenance, and support handoffs. Sovereign Mind may be relevant where that friction requires trusted, source-grounded answers rather than generic cloud AI.

This is an internal hypothesis. Public-facing copy must not claim a specific company has this pain unless the source explicitly says so.

## Agreement

All three council voices agreed on:

- Lead with customer pain and operational friction, not "sovereign AI" or "agentic systems."
- Build a public-source ledger before outreach.
- Preserve URLs for every factual claim.
- Separate sourced facts, public positioning, internal hypotheses, and open questions.
- Rank targets by conversation-worthiness, not company size or AI buzzwords.
- Require stronger evidence for Tier 1 targets than for longlist entries.
- Treat large accounts such as Krones, Syntegon, MULTIVAC, BOBST, GEA, and Bühler as benchmark accounts first.
- Use a diagnostic first-touch ask, not a demo pitch.
- Make service, lifecycle, digital service, sales engineering, customer success, or operations leaders the initial buyer/persona map.

## Disagreement

The main disagreement is how narrow the first ICP should be.

- GTM view: start tightly with packaging and food/pharma process-line builders because the pain is legible and customer-facing.
- Research-ops view: narrow the universe first, but keep the workflow rigorous enough to avoid bias toward familiar names.
- Skeptic view: keep a secondary path for adjacent service-heavy equipment makers if public evidence is stronger than in the named beachhead.

Decision: use a primary ICP plus a secondary ICP. The primary ICP gets the first benchmark pass. The secondary ICP can enter Tier 1 only if the evidence and contactability are stronger than primary-ICP candidates.

A second disagreement is the "two independent public signals" rule.

- Strong rule: every priority target should have at least two independent signals.
- Nuanced rule: longlist candidates can enter with one strong signal, but Tier 1 outreach requires two independent public signals.

Decision: require two independent signals for Tier 1 outreach; allow one-signal accounts into Tier 2 or watchlist with clear evidence gaps.

## Rejected Alternatives

- Generic "AI transformation for machine builders" campaign. Rejected because it violates the pain-first constraint and invites vendor fatigue.
- Buying or scraping a generic lead list. Rejected because it weakens source traceability and conversation quality.
- Ranking by company size or revenue. Rejected because large companies may be slower, less reachable, or already saturated with vendors.
- Ranking by "AI readiness." Rejected unless the target itself publicly discusses AI or digital-service initiatives.
- EU Data Act or compliance fear campaign. Rejected as a primary wedge because it risks sounding legalistic or speculative; compliance can remain a secondary context where sources support it.
- Predictive-maintenance pitch. Rejected as a first wedge because the category is crowded and can imply measurable outcomes Sovereign Mind should not claim without proof.

## Known Risks

- Public evidence may reflect marketing positioning, not actual operational pain.
- Large benchmark accounts may bias the campaign toward enterprise problems that do not generalize to reachable mid-market firms.
- A two-signal rule can still create false confidence if both signals derive from the same narrative.
- If the outreach is over-guarded, it may become too bland to earn replies.
- If the outreach is too personalized, it may feel intrusive.
- German-language nuance may matter for Mittelstand outreach.
- "Sovereign Mind" may sound abstract unless translated into customer-safe operational language.
- The current offer after the conversation is not fully specified: research interview, workshop, pilot, advisory, software prototype, or design partnership.

## Targeting Plan

### Primary ICP

DACH-based or DACH-rooted builders of:

- Packaging machinery.
- Food and beverage processing equipment.
- Pharma process-line equipment.
- Filling, inspection, labeling, and end-of-line automation.
- Service-heavy production equipment for regulated or uptime-sensitive environments.

### Secondary ICP

Adjacent machinery firms with strong public evidence of:

- Installed-base service.
- Remote support.
- Lifecycle services.
- Customer portals.
- Training academies.
- Technical documentation burden.
- Regulated customer environments.
- Digital service offerings.

### Three-Pass Workflow

1. Benchmark pass: build 6-10 benchmark dossiers from known leaders to calibrate language and pain signals.
2. Discovery pass: identify 40-60 public-source candidates across DACH.
3. Qualification pass: rank 20-30 targets and select 10-15 priority dossiers for Miguel/Reto review.

## Ranking Scorecard

Score each criterion 0-5. Double-weight customer pain visibility and conversation accessibility.

- Customer pain visibility: public evidence around downtime, scarce skilled labor, service burden, training, parts, maintenance, changeovers, OEE, compliance, or documentation.
- Installed-base complexity: lifecycle service, remote support, modernization, spare parts, technical documentation, global fleet, or long equipment lifetime.
- Aftermarket/service importance: service contracts, customer portals, technical support, training, or modernization programs.
- International support burden: global customers, distributor/service partner networks, multilingual support.
- Evidence quality: primary source, recency, specificity, and independence.
- Timing signal: recent service launch, digital initiative, job posts, trade-fair theme, support center, or restructuring.
- Conversation accessibility: named leaders, visible contact paths, events, warm routes.
- Sovereign Mind relevance: source-grounded, offline, trusted knowledge workflow could plausibly matter.
- Outreach safety: message can be written without unsupported claims.

## Reusable Dossier Schema

Each target dossier should include:

- Company name.
- Country, headquarters, region.
- Segment and machine category.
- Core machines/lines.
- Customer industries.
- Size indicators with URLs where public.
- Installed-base/service indicators.
- Public pain signals.
- Digital/service initiatives.
- Parts/support/training/modernization offerings.
- Compliance/regulatory hooks.
- Recent trigger events.
- Likely buyer personas.
- Named possible champions if public.
- Evidence table with source URLs, page title, source type, publication date, access date, quoted fact, paraphrased fact, and claim-safety notes.
- Public-safe facts.
- Internal hypotheses, explicitly labeled.
- Claims to avoid.
- Suggested outreach angle.
- First-call discovery questions.
- Objections to expect.
- Scorecard breakdown.
- Confidence rating.
- Recommended next action.
- Review status: unchecked, reviewed, approved for outreach.

## Public Copy Rules

Allowed phrasing:

- "Your public materials emphasize lifecycle service, support, training, or digital service."
- "We are speaking with machine builders about where installed-base support, documentation, and customer-facing service workflows create friction."
- "We are not assuming this is a priority for you; we are testing whether the pattern is real."

Avoid:

- "You are struggling with service."
- "Your customers are demanding AI."
- "You need sovereign AI."
- "We can reduce downtime by X percent."
- Any statement implying access to internal data, telemetry, customer contracts, or non-public financial stress.
