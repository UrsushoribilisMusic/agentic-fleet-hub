# Round 2 Deliberations: Sovereign Mind DACH Prospect Intelligence

Round 2 instruction: critique the other Round 1 proposals, revise recommendations, and stop after this round.

## Agent 1: GTM Planner Revision

### Agreement

The research operation and skeptic guardrails are necessary. The target list should not be ranked by "AI readiness"; it should be ranked by conversation-worthiness: visible customer pain, installed-base/service complexity, contactability, and ability to write safe outreach.

### Disagreement / Nuance

The "two independent public signals" rule should be required for Tier 1, not for every longlist account. Some promising private or mid-sized firms may have sparse public footprints.

Company marketing is not proof of pain, but it is useful evidence of how the company frames customer value. Label it as a public-positioning signal, not confirmed pain.

### Revised Recommendation

Run FCP-8 as a customer-conversation campaign supported by a public-evidence research operation. Start with DACH packaging, food/pharma processing, and adjacent service-heavy machine builders.

Tier 1 accounts require:

- At least two independent public signals of customer pain or service complexity.
- Preserved source URLs.
- Source ledger separating company claims, third-party context, job posts, reports, and news.
- Public-safe outreach angle using only sourced facts.
- Internal hypothesis clearly marked as unproven.
- Conversation ask aimed at learning, not selling.

### Remaining Risks

- Public evidence may reflect positioning, not actual pain.
- Large machine builders may already have internal programs.
- Tight evidence rules may bias toward public companies and famous brands.
- Excess caution could produce strong dossiers but too few conversations.

### Confidence

Medium-high on campaign shape; medium on account priority until evidence confirms current pain signals and reachable buyer paths.

## Agent 2: Research Operations Revision

### Agreement

The GTM planner's ICP narrowing is right. Packaging, food/pharma process-line builders, and adjacent service-heavy machinery are a more useful pilot scope than DACH machine builders broadly.

The skeptic is also right that the campaign should be judged by whether Miguel and Reto get 8-12 real exploratory conversations.

### Disagreement / Revision

Round 1 was too broad operationally. The scorecard should apply after a narrow seed universe is chosen. Company size should be downgraded as a targeting factor; visible aftermarket/service motion and reachable leadership matter more.

### Revised Recommendation

Run FCP-8 as a diagnostic-conversation campaign.

- ICP: DACH-based or DACH-rooted machine/process-line builders serving food, beverage, pharma, packaging, and adjacent regulated manufacturing environments.
- Primary pain lens: helping customers keep complex production lines running, supported, documented, commissioned, maintained, and staffed.
- First-touch framing: "We are studying how machine builders reduce customer friction across service, documentation, training, and installed-base support."
- Avoid: "AI transformation," "you have a knowledge problem," or claims that a target is struggling.
- Success metric: 8-12 exploratory conversations with service, aftermarket, digital service, sales engineering, or customer success leaders.
- Output: ranked 25-40 target list, 10-15 priority dossiers, reusable source ledger, and conversation guide.

### Remaining Risks

- Seed accounts may be too large and vendor-saturated.
- Diagnostic language can become too vague unless tied to concrete workflows.
- DACH outreach may need German-native nuance.

### Confidence

High that narrowing the ICP improves the pilot; medium on exact seed-account mix.

## Agent 3: Skeptic / Risk Reviewer Revision

### Agreement

The ICP narrowing is directionally right, and the source-led scorecard is essential. Tier 1 should require at least two independent public signals, and all outreach hypotheses should be marked as hypotheses.

### Disagreement / Adjustment

Do not make the ICP so narrow that stronger adjacent service-heavy equipment makers are excluded too early. The real constraint should be installed-base service complexity plus public evidence of digital/service friction or ambition.

Treat large seed accounts as benchmark accounts, not necessarily top outreach targets. They are useful for pattern extraction; first outreach may perform better with mid-sized firms adjacent to those leaders.

### Revised Recommendation

Run the pilot for:

- Primary ICP: DACH-based packaging machinery, food processing, pharma process-line, inspection, filling, labeling, end-of-line automation, and service-heavy production equipment builders.
- Secondary ICP: adjacent machinery firms with strong public evidence of installed-base service, remote support, lifecycle services, regulated customer environments, or digital service offerings.

Build the list in three passes:

1. Benchmark pass: analyze 6-10 known leaders to define signal patterns and language.
2. Discovery pass: identify 40-60 DACH candidates from public sources.
3. Qualification pass: rank 20-30 targets, with Tier 1 requiring at least two independent public signals.

### Remaining Risks

- The ICP may still be too broad unless "process-line" and "service-heavy" are defined operationally.
- Large benchmark accounts may bias toward enterprise problems.
- Two signals can still create false confidence if they derive from the same press narrative.
- The "Sovereign Mind" concept needs translation into customer-safe language.

### Confidence

Medium-high on direction; medium until benchmark dossiers show repeatable pain signals and reachable buyer paths.
