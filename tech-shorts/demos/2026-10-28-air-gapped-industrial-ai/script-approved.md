# Approved narration — air-gapped industrial AI demo

Approved by Miguel in chat, 28 October 2026. Opening and ending preserved.
Product names deliberately omitted. Visual directions are not spoken.

[01 · Cave paintings → Trojan horse → Leonardo’s machine drawing → Wikipedia]

Life is about stories, and we have always used stories to transmit knowledge.

From how to hunt, to how to outsmart an opponent, to how to build a machine. Over time, we organized that knowledge into encyclopedias—and made it available to everyone.

AI now lets us converse with that knowledge.

[02 · iPhone: technical question, answer, and supporting source]

Our product puts that capability in the hands of industrial technicians—even where cloud AI cannot go.

Open-weight models run directly on the device, using local knowledge packs built from technical manuals, operating procedures, and expertise—including how to interpret machine logs.

Inference works offline, without per-query cloud fees or sending questions and proprietary knowledge outside the customer’s infrastructure. Customers control their models, their knowledge, and their deployment.

[03 · Animated feedback diagram, following each connection]

The knowledge base improves through two feedback channels.

Technicians can flag unhelpful advice. Our application also exposes disposition signals derived from Jacobian-based analysis of the model’s internal response dynamics. We use these signals to identify interactions where the available knowledge may be insufficient.

[04 · Backend: proposed improvement → editable Wiki → reviewer approval]

Larger models running on customer premises process this feedback and propose improvements to the knowledge base.

Experts review those suggestions in a familiar, editable Wiki. They approve or modify changes before updated knowledge reaches technicians. A human always controls what gets deployed.

Field experience becomes knowledge the whole organization can reuse.

[05 · Settings: model selection and knowledge packs → camera/OCR demonstration]

Our application supports multiple open-weight models and knowledge packs. It can also use the camera to read available text and bring visual information into the interaction.

[06 · Return to the technician’s workflow]

An industrial abrasives distributor is already testing the system, and we are in discussions with equipment manufacturers in Switzerland and Germany.

The business case is concrete: shorter setup times, fewer unnecessary repairs, and fewer repeat customer visits.

That reduces service costs and improves our customers’ bottom line. Our product should pay for itself through the time and visits it saves.
