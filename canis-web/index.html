Transparency is the product.

Closed APIs can return polished text. Open-weight models let us inspect the mechanics behind that text. Canis turns that advantage into a compact interface for model-state inspection.

Purposeful AI

Open weights make the signal possible

Canis reads hidden-state direction and next-token uncertainty instead of asking the model to self-report. That is only possible when the model can run locally and expose its internal tensors.

Sovereign by design

No overseas inference round-trip

The iOS app runs smartphone-class models with MLX. The Docker prototype can run Apertus for reproducible review. Prompts, sources, chunks, and answers stay inside the deployment boundary.

Commercial value

A new parameter for trust

Enterprises can use disposition as an additional signal for escalation queues, answer review, model comparison, governance logs, and safer deployment of open models.

Six readable states.

The dog face is not a claim that the model has feelings. It is an instrument panel for a measured disposition signal: concept-vector alignment plus uncertainty.

Confident The answer is stable and the model appears to converge cleanly.
Unsure The response shows hesitation or high uncertainty.
Curious The model is opening a line of thought or looking for context.
Concerned The answer may involve risk, harm, or a safety boundary.
Reluctant The model leans toward refusal, caution, or constrained output.
Warm The response carries supportive or friendly alignment.

How the readout works.

Canis adapts the Jacobian lens idea into a forward-only mobile pipeline: read a mid-layer hidden state, project it through a cached J-space matrix, compare it to disposition anchors, and gate the result with entropy.

J-space probe

Not keyword matching

The readout is computed from the model's internal activation geometry before the final answer fully streams. Our paper documents calibration experiments, controls, and why some candidate axes were removed.

Practical runtime

Forward-only, cheap enough for edge AI

The production path avoids a second generation pass. On iOS, MLX and Apple Silicon make the app much faster than the Docker demo, which favors reproducibility over speed.

Your documents, local answers.

Sources turn Canis into a private assistant for manuals, notes, policies, and reference PDFs. The app chunks documents locally, retrieves relevant passages, and cites the sections used in the answer.

Local RAG

Small models become more useful when the context is yours.

Instead of sending private files to a remote API, Canis keeps the document workflow on the device. This is a strong use case for local models: focused answers, visible citations, and a disposition signal when the model is confident, uncertain, or constrained.
