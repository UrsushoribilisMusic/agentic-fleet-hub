## Two things I tried and threw away
Looked for a way to avoid translating ~118k words. Built two classifiers, **both wrong**:
1. *Duplicate detector* — premise was that short table fragments already exist in the
   corpus attached to their part numbers. Flagged 26 lines, and the wrong ones: it
   wanted to drop "1. increasing the work speed, or 2. increasing traverse speed…",
   which is real guidance. Cause: `-layout` puts a wide table row and the prose beside
   it on the *same* visual line, so prose adjacent to a table reads as "coded".
2. *Pass-through detector* — premise was that proper-noun-only lines need no
   translation. Wanted to pass through "Roughing / Semi-Finish / High Finish" and
   "Silicon Carbide", which are precisely the terms a Spanish speaker searches by
   (desbaste, semiacabado, alto brillo).

Conclusion: no shortcut exists; the terse lines are often the densest search
vocabulary. Only genuine junk dropped — 11 lines of dot-leader contents pages and one
line reading "Solid" 52 times.
