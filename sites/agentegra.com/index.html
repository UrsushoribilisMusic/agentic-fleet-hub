The problem

The knowledge exists. It just isn't reachable.

Every machine builder we've worked with has the same four problems, and none of them are AI problems.

It's scattered

Manuals in one system, service bulletins in another, the real answers in tickets, emails, and one engineer's memory.

It's leaving

The people who commissioned the machines in 2009 are retiring, and what they know was never written down in a form anyone else can use.

It's needed offline

A field engineer in a basement plant does not have reliable connectivity, and the answer is needed now.

It doesn't need to go to a US-hosted model

Proprietary machine documentation, customer process data and tolerances are not going into a US-hosted model, whatever the terms of service say.

The approach

Open-weight AI, on your side of the firewall.

your docs & logs → compile → cited wiki + open-weight modelApertus · Mistral → runs on your hardware, offline

We compile your documents, logs and tickets into a cited, auditable knowledge base, and pair it with open-weight AI that runs on your own hardware: Apertus, the fully-open Swiss model, and Mistral. No US cloud, and no data leaving the perimeter. The wiki holds the facts with a source trail an auditor can follow; the model reads and reasons over them, on-premise or fully offline in the field.

How we work

Four steps. You own what comes out.

01 — AUDIT

Find out what's answerable today

We start with a scoped engagement to find out where your machine knowledge actually lives, what condition it's in, and what a cited assistant could answer today. You get a written assessment and a working demo running on your own documents, not on a sample dataset.

Scope and duration are set per customer. It is deliberately small enough to approve without a committee.

scoped per customer
02 — BUILD

AI compiles your knowledge into a cited wiki

We use AI to compile your documents, logs and tickets into a structured, cross-referenced wiki that regenerates as the sources change, with provenance behind every claim. The heavy generation runs in the cloud for speed, or fully on your premises when the corpus is too sensitive to leave the building.

We start from a core we've already built and harden it against your case. You are not paying us to invent a RAG pipeline from scratch, and you are not getting someone else's roadmap either.

cloud or on-prem generation
03 — HAND OVER

Local inference on hardware you own

You get a system, not a seat licence. Day-to-day queries run with local inference on the hardware you already have, so nothing leaves the building and there is no per-token cloud bill that grows every time your team uses it. It keeps working if we disappear, and gets better as it ingests more of your sources.

local inference, no token cost
04 — IMPROVE

It gets sharper the more you use it

Every answer can be rated. A thumbs-down, or a correction from the person who actually knows, becomes a new cited entry in the corpus. The wiki fills its own gaps, so the system improves over time instead of going stale, and the knowledge compounds as your team works.

feedback becomes corpus

What we build on

The core already exists. The work is fitting it to you.

Sovereign Mind

Upload documents, get a readable cited wiki, and query it fully offline on a phone. Open-weight models (Apertus or Mistral), a citation on every answer, zero bytes leaving the device. This is the starting point for most engagements, tailored to the customer's corpus, vocabulary and workflow rather than shipped as-is.

The Automated Technical File

A pipeline that turns source code and operational logs into a cited, regenerable technical file, mapped to EU AI Act Articles 12, 13 and 14. Running in production on a physical machine with over 50,000 logged events.

Flotilla

Our open multi-agent orchestration framework, with cross-model peer review and circuit breakers. It's how a very small team delivers at this pace, and it's documented in a research paper on failure modes rather than in a brochure.
